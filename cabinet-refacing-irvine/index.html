Decide what you want to change

Cabinet Refacing Irvine is a service to discuss when you’re considering a different cabinet appearance without beginning with a full cabinet replacement plan. Start by naming the parts of the kitchen that bother you most. Is it the door style, the visible finish, or the overall look alongside the counters and floor? Being clear about the goal helps you compare refacing with other options.

Look at the cabinets in daylight and note visible wear, loose parts, or damage. Don’t assume that a new exterior finish will solve issues with how cabinets function. If storage, access, or the arrangement is also a concern, mention that before settling on a direction.

What the discussion should cover

Refacing decisions depend on the existing cabinets and the result you’re after. A useful first review can include the visible surfaces, cabinet layout, and any functional concerns. It can also help to consider how a change in cabinet appearance may relate to counters, tile, or flooring if those are part of your plans.

Before you meet, use this short checklist:

  • Take clear photos of the cabinet doors, drawer fronts, and the wider kitchen.
  • Note visible damage, loose hardware, or places that are difficult to use.
  • Identify what you want to look different and what you’re comfortable keeping.
  • Gather a few design examples, then describe the details you like about them.
  • List any other remodeling work you’re considering at the same time.

Avoid treating refacing and replacement as interchangeable terms. Ask for a clear explanation of what the proposed scope includes, and don’t overlook functional problems just because the visible finish is your main concern. If the cabinet arrangement itself isn’t working, bring that up early.
